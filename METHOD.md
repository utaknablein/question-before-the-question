# The Vorfrage method

Vorfrage checks a question before anyone spends time answering it. This document explains the method so a person can run it without software, and so the software can be judged against it.

## Principles

1. **Offer, never block.** The person can always answer their original question. The job is to make the choice visible, not to take it away.
2. **Stay quiet when the question is good.** Most questions do not need reframing. A tool that reframes everything becomes noise within a week.
3. **Be specific.** Every premise and every reframe must point at something in the question as written.
4. **Respect the asker.** A reframe is not a correction. It is a second option, laid next to the first.
5. **Record the choice.** Which reframe a person picks, and what they decided next, is how the method learns.

## Step 0: Triage

Before reframing, decide whether the question deserves it. Reframe when at least two of these are true:

- The answer will shape a decision that is expensive or hard to reverse.
- The question contains a built-in solution ("which vendor", "how do we build", "how do we cut").
- The question has a number or target that someone else set.
- The person asking is not the person who owns the decision.
- The question starts with "how do I get them to...".

Do not reframe factual lookups, drafting requests, or questions that already name a goal, a measure and an owner.

## Steps 1 to 5

| Step | Output | Test of a good output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Restate | The question as asked, in one neutral sentence | The asker would say "yes, that is my question" |
| 2. Name the decision | What they are trying to decide, and who owns it | It is a decision, not a topic |
| 3. Surface premises | Two to four things the question takes as true, rated High, Medium or Low dependency | Each could be checked; High means a useful answer depends on it |
| 4. Reframe | Three questions, each using a different shift, each saying what it would change | Each is a question the asker could actually ask tomorrow |
| 5. Pick the first | The question to settle first, with one sentence of why | Answering it would change how you answer the others |

## The seven shifts

**Upstream.** What goal is this question serving, and is there a better route to it? Use when the question names a solution. *"Which chatbot vendor?" becomes "Why are customers contacting us at all?"*

**Premise.** What must be true for this question to make sense? Use when the question assumes a cause. *"How do we build features to fix churn?" becomes "Which customers leave, and why?"*

**Scope.** Is this too narrow, or too broad, to act on? Use when the question is a slogan or a detail. *"How do we adopt AI faster?" becomes "Which three workflows would change most?"*

**Owner.** Whose question is this, and are they the one asking? Use when the question is about someone else's behavior or decision. *"Should we hire a Chief AI Officer?" becomes "Which AI decisions fall between chairs today?"*

**Timing.** Why now, and what changes if it waits? Use when urgency arrives from outside. *"Should we announce an AI strategy this quarter?" becomes "What breaks if we wait six months?"*

**Measure.** What would count as a good answer, and who decides? Use when success is undefined. *"How do we improve engagement?" becomes "Which number, for whom, by how much?"*

**Inversion.** What should we stop, or what would make the opposite true? Use when every option is additive. *"What should we build?" becomes "What should we stop?"*

## Running it with a team (45 minutes)

1. Each person writes one real question they asked AI, or were asked, this month. (5 min)
2. In pairs, run triage. Keep the questions that pass. (5 min)
3. Each pair runs steps 1 to 5 on one question, using the worksheet in the demo. (20 min)
4. Share the pairs where the question before the question changed the plan. (10 min)
5. Agree one question the team will reframe before it goes to the next leadership meeting. (5 min)

## What Vorfrage is not

- It is not a devil's advocate. A devil's advocate challenges the answer; Vorfrage challenges the question.
- It is not a clarifying-question feature. Clarifying questions collect missing detail to answer the same question better. Vorfrage asks whether it is the right question.
- It is not a judgment on the asker. The best leaders ask the wrong question often; they just check before acting on the answer.
